My small RLHF experiment beat random choice. A simpler ranker still won.

I built a Python project that chooses between two existing assistant replies. The data comes from Anthropic HH-RLHF, helpful-base: real answer pairs ranked by people.

I adapted Chapter 11 of Dipesh Gautam's Reinforcement Learning: learn a reward from human choices, then train a selector. A KL penalty keeps its choices closer to the starting policy. A separate Chapter 12.1 demo shows teacher forcing on two synthetic arithmetic examples.

The split: 8,000 reward-training pairs, 4,000 policy-training pairs, and 2,000 unseen test pairs.

Chance of selecting the human-preferred reply:
• Random choice: 50.0%
• Direct reward ranker: 60.6%
• RLHF without a KL penalty: 59.1%
• RLHF with KL weight 0.2: 58.4%
• RLHF with KL weight 1.0: 55.4%

The RLHF results average three training seeds. Two full runs produced identical metrics.

More KL penalty reduced drift, but also reduced preference selection. Extra training stages did not beat direct ranking in this experiment.

It selects existing replies; it does not generate answers. It scores replies without reading the prompt, so it may learn writing style rather than helpfulness in context.
